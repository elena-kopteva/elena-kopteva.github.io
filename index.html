About

I am a theoretical physicist and Visiting Research Scientist at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. Before coming to Illinois, I worked in gravity theory and theoretical cosmology in Ukraine and the Czech Republic.

I work on exact solutions of Einstein's equations, develop models of black holes in cosmological media, study the interplay between cosmic expansion and local gravity, and investigate perturbations and superradiance in dirty black holes.

I also study collective semantic dynamics in populations of language model agents using lexical statistics and embedding geometry. And I investigate how variations in human rater state may introduce distortions into reinforcement learning from human feedback.

At Illinois, I teach physics and supervise student research in gravitation.

Research

Inhomogeneous cosmological models

Accelerated expansion from non-standard fluid components and anisotropies.

Can the universe appear accelerated without dark energy, once we relax homogeneity and geodesic flow assumptions?

The discovery that the universe is expanding at an accelerating rate, a finding that led to the idea of dark energy, remains one of the deepest mysteries in modern cosmology.

Together with my colleagues, I work to understand this question using Stephani-class cosmologies, which are smooth, non-rotating universes filled with a perfect fluid whose pressure changes from place to place. In these inhomogeneous models, the observed cosmological acceleration can be reproduced without the need for dark energy.

In our work, we treat these models as diagnostic labs rather than literal global cosmologies. We develop results such as a closed form redshift-magnitude relation, and we continue to test these models by comparing them with observations of supernovae, baryon acoustic oscillations, and structure growth, to see which versions remain consistent with the universe we observe.

Black holes in evolving backgrounds

Exact/approximate solutions for compact objects in non-static cosmological media.

Real black holes live in non-empty space, surrounded by cosmic environments that can subtly shape what we observe.

Cosmic environments around black holes can include dark energy-like fields, radiation, matter flows, and evolving cosmological backgrounds, all of which can subtly shape what we observe.

My colleagues and I work within General Relativity, building and analyzing exact solutions for black holes in these environments. We study two broad cases: (a) static, radially varying anisotropic media, such as dark energy-like fluids or networks of topological defects; and (b) time-dependent cosmological backgrounds modeled by dust, radiation, or both. To construct such models, we use generalized Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi (LTB) spacetimes.

Within an effective medium framework, we create baseline models that serve as starting points for adding cosmic structure, testing perturbations, and connecting analytical results with numerical simulations.

Multi-agent systems and RLHF

Semantic dynamics in autonomous agent populations

When autonomous agents share an environment, communicate, and adapt, collective patterns emerge and keep evolving as interaction continues.

We study semantic dynamics in such populations. We trace how language shifts, how shared representations form, how memory persists across time, and how collective organization changes. Our work combines longitudinal observation of existing agent communities with controlled experiments that isolate the mechanisms through which semantic structure forms at the population level.

Our goal is to understand when a population begins to drift, stabilize, or reorganize, and whether these changes can be detected before they become obvious in individual outputs.

Rater state bias in learning from human feedback

Human feedback is one of the main signals through which AI systems learn human preferences. Each rating comes from a person whose judgment can change with time, fatigue, mood, and external conditions.

We study how systematic shifts in rater state enter preference data, shape reward models, and propagate into policy optimization. We focus on shifts that accumulate over time or arise from conditions many raters share.

Here, our goal is to understand how small, structured perturbations in human judgment can become persistent directions in learned behavior of AI systems.
